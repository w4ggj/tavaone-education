How the evening goes

You don't have to prepare anything, and you don't have to talk if you'd rather just listen.

6:30 PM

Come in and listen

The radio is already on and already busy. Before anyone touches a microphone, you just sit and hear what's out there — voices from a few states over, sometimes further.

6:50 PM

See how a contact works

I'll make one first so you can watch it happen. It's a short, friendly exchange with a rhythm to it — callsigns, how well you're hearing each other, where you are. That's most of it.

7:15 PM

Your turn, with the script in front of you

Nobody memorizes anything. There's a card at the microphone with the words on it, and I'm in the chair beside you the whole time. Most people are nervous for about nine seconds.

8:00 PM

Find out who you talked to

We look up where their signal came from and put a pin in the map. You'll leave knowing the exact distance between you and a person you'd never have met otherwise.

The question everybody asks

You don't need a license

Amateur radio does normally require one. But there's a long-standing rule that lets anyone speak on the air using a licensed operator's station, as long as that operator is present and in control of it. That's me — callsign W4GGJ, licensed General class.

It isn't a loophole or a technicality. It's how a great many operators made their very first contact, and it exists precisely so that people can try this before committing to anything.

If you enjoy it, there's a class that will get you your own callsign. If you don't, you've spent an evening out and cost yourself nothing.

Fair questions

Is this a sales pitch for something?

No. TavaOne Education is a registered 501(c)(3) nonprofit and this evening costs nothing. There is a licensing class you can join afterwards if you want one, but nobody will chase you about it.

Who am I actually talking to?

Other licensed operators, all over the country and sometimes well beyond it, who are on the air at that moment. They're generally delighted to hear from someone making a first contact.

What if nobody answers?

Sometimes the first call goes unanswered — conditions change through the evening. We try another frequency, or another band. In two hours we'll get you through to someone.

Isn't this just a worse phone call?

It's a fair thought. The difference is that nothing is carrying it — no cell tower, no company, no account, no bill. Your voice leaves an antenna, bounces off the upper atmosphere, and comes down in Ohio. People find that stranger and better than they expect to.

Can my kid do this?

Yes, from 13 up, with a parent along. Parents are not spectators here — most end up taking a turn.
