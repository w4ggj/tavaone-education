The Evening

How the evening goes

You don't prepare anything, and you don't have to talk if you'd rather just listen. Here's the shape of it.

📻

Come in and listen

The radio is already on and already busy. Before anyone touches a microphone, you just sit and hear what's out there — voices from a few states over, sometimes further.

🎙️

Watch a contact happen

An Elmer makes one first so you can see it. It's a short, friendly exchange with a rhythm to it — callsigns, how well you're hearing each other, where you are. That's most of it.

🗣️

Your turn, with the script in front of you

Nobody memorizes anything. There's a card at the microphone with the words on it, and an Elmer in the chair beside you. Most people are nervous for about nine seconds.

🗺️

Find out who you reached

We look up where their signal came from and put a pin in the map. You leave knowing the exact distance between you and someone you'd never have met otherwise.

The question everybody asks

No license? No problem.

Amateur radio normally requires a license. But there's a long-standing rule that lets anyone speak on the air using a licensed operator's station, as long as that operator is present and in control of it. That's us — W4GGJ, licensed General class, right beside you.

It isn't a loophole or a technicality. It's how a great many operators made their very first contact, and it exists precisely so people can try this before committing to anything.

If you enjoy it, we'll get you your own callsign through our Technician License Course. If you don't, you've spent a good evening out and it cost you nothing.

Fair questions

The things people ask before they come

Is this a sales pitch for something?

No. TavaOne Education is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and the evening costs nothing. There's a licensing course you can join afterward if you want one — nobody will chase you about it.

Who am I actually talking to?

Other licensed operators, all over the country and sometimes well beyond it, who are on the air at that moment. They're generally delighted to hear from someone making a first contact.

What if nobody answers?

Sometimes the first call goes unanswered — conditions shift through the evening. We try another frequency, or another band. In two hours we'll get you through to someone.

Isn't this just a worse phone call?

Fair thought. The difference is that nothing is carrying it — no tower, no company, no account, no bill. Your voice leaves an antenna, bounces off the upper atmosphere, and comes down two states away. People find that stranger and better than they expect to.

Can my kid do this?

Yes, from 13 up, with a parent along. Parents aren't spectators here — most end up taking a turn.
